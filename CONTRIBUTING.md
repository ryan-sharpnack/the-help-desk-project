# Contributing to The Help Desk Project

First off, thank you for your interest in contributing to The Help Desk Project!

Our mission is to bridge the gap between learning IT and doing IT by creating an open-source community where aspiring professionals can develop practical technical skills through hands-on experience.

Every contribution matters. Whether you're sharing a resolved support ticket, showcasing your homelab, submitting a real-world troubleshooting scenario, or offering technical feedback, you're helping make this community more valuable.

## Ways to Contribute

There are several ways to get involved:

### 1. Submit Resolved IT Support Tickets

Share technical problems you've encountered and resolved in your homelab or personal environment.

We encourage contributors to document:

- Ticket title and description
- Difficulty level
- Technology or system involved
- Environment and configurations
- Symptoms and observations
- Troubleshooting methodology
- Root cause
- Resolution
- Verification
- Lessons learned

Your documentation should explain not only what you did, but why you did it.

### 2. Showcase Your Homelab

Share your homelab and explain how you're developing your technical capabilities.

Include relevant information such as:

- Hardware and infrastructure
- Virtualization platforms
- Operating systems
- Network architecture
- Applications and services
- Projects and configurations
- Skills you're developing

Screenshots, diagrams, and documentation are encouraged.

### 3. Submit Real-World IT Scenarios

Industry professionals, experienced technicians, and hiring managers are encouraged to share common IT support problems they encounter in professional environments.

These scenarios help aspiring professionals practice troubleshooting problems that reflect actual workplace expectations.

Do not include confidential company information, proprietary documentation, credentials, or personally identifiable information.

### 4. Improve Existing Contributions

You can help by:

- Correcting technical inaccuracies.
- Improving troubleshooting documentation.
- Suggesting alternative solutions.
- Adding verification steps.
- Providing constructive feedback.
- Updating outdated technical information.

### 5. Mentor and Support Contributors

Experienced professionals are encouraged to participate in discussions, review contributions, answer technical questions, and share industry knowledge.

Your experience can make a meaningful difference in someone's professional development.

Visit the **Mentorship** and **Industry Insights** categories in [Discussions](https://github.com/ryan-sharpnack/the-help-desk-project/discussions) to get started.

## How to Submit a Contribution

1. Fork this repository.
2. Clone your fork to your local environment.
3. Create a new branch for your contribution.
4. Add your ticket, homelab documentation, or improvement.
5. Review your submission for accuracy and completeness.
6. Commit and push your changes.
7. Open a Pull Request against the main repository.
8. Respond to feedback and make any requested improvements.

Have a question or not ready to submit yet? Join the conversation in [GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/ryan-sharpnack/the-help-desk-project/discussions). Use [Issues](https://github.com/ryan-sharpnack/the-help-desk-project/issues) for specific corrections or content fixes.

## Contribution Guidelines

To maintain a useful and professional knowledge base, please follow these guidelines:

- Be technically accurate and explain your methodology.
- Document solutions clearly enough for others to reproduce them.
- Include relevant commands and configuration examples when appropriate.
- Distinguish between verified solutions and proposed solutions.
- Never include passwords, API keys, access tokens, or other secrets.
- Never publish private information or confidential organizational data.
- Credit original sources when using external documentation or material.
- Be respectful and constructive when reviewing other contributions.

## Technical Documentation Standards

We encourage contributors to explain the reasoning behind their troubleshooting decisions.

A strong submission should demonstrate:

- Problem identification
- Technical investigation
- Logical troubleshooting
- Root cause analysis
- Solution implementation
- Verification and validation
- Clear technical communication

The goal isn't simply to demonstrate that something works. It's to demonstrate that you understand why it works.

## Pull Request Expectations

Before submitting a Pull Request:

- Ensure your contribution follows the repository's structure.
- Check spelling, formatting, and documentation quality.
- Remove sensitive information.
- Verify commands and configurations where possible.
- Provide a concise description of your contribution.

Maintainers may request clarification, corrections, or additional documentation before accepting a contribution.

## Community Participation

All contributors are expected to follow our [Code of Conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).

We welcome people of all experience levels. Beginners should feel comfortable asking questions, and experienced professionals should feel encouraged to share their knowledge.

Constructive feedback is encouraged. Disrespectful behavior is not.

## Thank You!

The Help Desk Project grows through the collective contributions of its community.

Whether you're submitting your first ticket or sharing decades of industry experience, thank you for helping make practical IT experience more accessible to everyone.
