# The Help Desk Project

💬 [Join the community in Discussions](https://github.com/ryan-sharpnack/the-help-desk-project/discussions)

### Don't just learn IT. Practice IT. Prove it.

**An open-source, community-driven initiative bridging the gap between learning IT and doing IT.**

Welcome to The Help Desk Project!

Breaking into the IT industry can be challenging. Candidates spend countless hours studying textbooks, completing courses, earning certifications, and preparing for interviews. Yet, when they finally land their first opportunity, they are often expected to troubleshoot unfamiliar problems, navigate enterprise environments, and resolve technical issues with minimal supervision.

There is a gap between knowing IT and actually doing IT.

**We're here to help close that gap.**

The Help Desk Project is a community where aspiring IT professionals develop practical, hands-on experience by building homelabs, working through real-world IT support scenarios, documenting resolved tickets, and learning from experienced industry professionals.

Our objective is simple: help people develop the technical competencies, troubleshooting methodologies, and professional habits employers expect from day one.

---

## Our Mission

To make practical IT experience more accessible by building an open-source community where people can learn by doing, demonstrate their technical abilities, and connect with professionals who can help guide their development.

We believe that preparing for an IT career should involve more than memorizing technical concepts or following step-by-step tutorials.

It should involve identifying problems, investigating symptoms, troubleshooting systems, implementing solutions, validating results, and documenting the entire process.

We want contributors to graduate from simply learning how technology works to demonstrating that they can work with technology.

## Who Is This For?

The Help Desk Project is built around three communities.

### 1. Aspiring IT Professionals

Are you trying to break into IT? Already studying for certifications? Building your first homelab?

This community is for you.

- Build and showcase your homelab.
- Work through practical IT support scenarios.
- Document troubleshooting methodologies and resolutions.
- Learn technologies used in professional IT environments.
- Develop technical documentation and communication skills.
- Build a portfolio demonstrating practical competencies.
- Receive feedback and mentorship from industry professionals.

You don't need professional IT experience to participate. You just need a willingness to learn, experiment, document, and contribute.

### 2. IT Professionals and Industry Veterans

Have experience working in IT? Help the next generation develop the skills that matter.

- Share common support tickets and troubleshooting scenarios.
- Offer technical guidance and mentorship.
- Review submitted ticket resolutions.
- Share industry knowledge and best practices.
- Recommend technologies and competencies aspiring professionals should develop.
- Help contributors understand how IT operates in professional environments.

Your experience can help someone become better prepared for their first opportunity.

### 3. Hiring Managers and IT Leaders

Help us understand what employers actually expect from entry-level IT professionals.

- Share frequently encountered help desk tickets.
- Identify recurring technical problems within your organization.
- Recommend relevant technologies and tools.
- Highlight the competencies that distinguish strong candidates.
- Provide feedback on the technical readiness of contributors.

We want to build learning experiences around the problems people actually encounter in the workplace.

**Help us prepare candidates for the work they will actually be expected to perform.**

## How It Works

The concept is straightforward.

**Build. Troubleshoot. Document. Share. Improve.**

1. **Build:** Create a homelab or use an existing personal environment to develop practical IT skills.
2. **Troubleshoot:** Work through realistic IT support scenarios and technical problems.
3. **Document:** Record the troubleshooting process, root cause, resolution, and verification steps.
4. **Share:** Contribute your tickets, homelab projects, and technical documentation to the community.
5. **Improve:** Receive feedback, learn from others, and continuously develop your technical capabilities.

Every documented ticket becomes an opportunity for someone else to learn.

Every homelab becomes an opportunity to demonstrate practical experience.

Every contribution makes this community more valuable.

## Contributing to the Project

There are several ways to get involved.

### Submit a Resolved Ticket

Document an IT support issue you have encountered and resolved in your homelab.

We encourage contributors to follow a consistent documentation format so that others can understand the problem, reproduce the scenario, and learn from the resolution.

**Recommended ticket documentation:**

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Ticket Title | Brief description of the issue |
| Difficulty | Beginner, Intermediate, or Advanced |
| Category | Networking, Windows, Linux, Hardware, etc. |
| Environment | Systems, technologies, and configurations involved |
| Problem Description | What happened and what was reported? |
| Symptoms | What did you observe? |
| Troubleshooting | What steps did you take to investigate? |
| Root Cause | What caused the problem? |
| Resolution | How did you resolve the issue? |
| Verification | How did you confirm the resolution worked? |
| Lessons Learned | What did you learn from the experience? |

Include relevant commands, configuration examples, screenshots, and supporting documentation when appropriate.

### Showcase Your Homelab

Your homelab is your personal IT training environment.

We encourage contributors to document and share:

- Hardware and virtualization infrastructure.
- Operating systems and server configurations.
- Network topology and infrastructure.
- Active Directory and identity management.
- Windows and Linux administration.
- Cloud and hybrid environments.
- Automation and scripting.
- Security configurations and monitoring.

Explain what you built, why you built it, what technologies you used, and what you learned.

### Share Real-World IT Scenarios

Industry professionals and hiring managers are encouraged to contribute common IT support scenarios.

These scenarios can become practical exercises for aspiring professionals to reproduce and resolve in their own environments.

Please ensure that submissions contain no confidential company information, personally identifiable information, credentials, or proprietary documentation.

### Offer Mentorship and Feedback

Technical knowledge becomes even more valuable when shared.

Help contributors improve their troubleshooting processes, technical documentation, and understanding of professional IT practices.

Constructive feedback is always welcome.

## Community Principles

We want to build a community that values:

- **Practical Experience:** Prioritize hands-on learning and demonstrable technical competencies.
- **Knowledge Sharing:** Encourage collaboration and the exchange of technical knowledge.
- **Professionalism:** Communicate respectfully and document work clearly.
- **Continuous Improvement:** Treat mistakes and troubleshooting challenges as learning opportunities.
- **Industry Relevance:** Align projects and exercises with technologies and problems encountered in professional IT environments.
- **Accessibility:** Welcome contributors regardless of their experience level or educational background.

This is a community built around helping people succeed.

## Our Vision

We envision a community where aspiring IT professionals can demonstrate what they are capable of doing before they ever walk into their first IT job.

Ultimately, we want The Help Desk Project to become a valuable resource for anyone preparing to enter the IT industry and for the professionals committed to helping them succeed.
